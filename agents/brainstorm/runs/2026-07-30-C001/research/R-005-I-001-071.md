## Idea echoed
- **Title:** Blind-flight withheld-intent probe — discoverability measured from a stripped serialization.
- **Description:** Serialize a screen to structure (element tree, control names, reading order) with identifying giveaways stripped (route, title, comments, copy); the reviewer must state the screen's purpose and primary action from structure alone. Gap between inferred and actual purpose is the discoverability finding.
- **Riskiest assumption:** That an LLM's inference from a stripped serialization correlates with *human* discoverability, rather than reflecting the model's own parsing limits.
- **Warrant:** Inferring purpose from structure is a task the eyeless loop can genuinely perform, and its failure is self-evidencing.

## Steelman (1 paragraph)
The loop has no eyes and no browser (brief §Context), so it cannot run a real five-second test. This idea finds the one first-impression signal that survives that constraint: purpose-inference is a *text* task an LLM can honestly attempt on a serialized DOM. It borrows a validated human instrument — the five-second/first-impression test explicitly measures "core understanding of purpose" [1][4] — and inverts the sycophancy problem structurally: withholding intent means the reviewer cannot pattern-match to the spec, so a wrong guess is hard to fake. As a *punch-list reachability* check (is every promised affordance in the tree?) it is nearly unimpeachable and needs no human-correlation claim at all.

## Toulmin decomposition
- **Claim:** An LLM's failure to infer a screen's purpose from a stripped serialization is a valid signal of a human discoverability defect.
- **Grounds:** (a) Five-second/first-impression testing is an established instrument measuring whether users grasp a page's purpose [1][4]. (b) A stripped serialization is producible in this repo — `src/app/spaces/page.tsx:97,113,123,138` shows purpose lives in `<h1>`, sub-copy, placeholder, button label; strip them and a legible "form + card-grid index" structure remains. (c) BUT LLM-vs-human alignment on this class of task is weak: GPT first-click predictions differed significantly from real human data in 53% of tasks (n=3431) [2]; LLM heuristic evaluators catch only ~21–32% of human-found issues with hallucinated false positives [3][5]; synthetic evaluation is stochastic/unreliable across repeated prompts [6].
- **Warrant:** Purpose-inference is eyeless-doable and self-evidencing.
- **Backing:** MeasuringU/first-impression literature validates purpose-comprehension as a construct [1].
- **Qualifier:** Holds *only* as a one-directional, structure-level punch-list ("promised affordance unreachable"); the human-correlation claim is unsupported.
- **Rebuttal:** The probe removes exactly what humans see (rendered copy/layout) and feeds a modality (stripped tree) no human ever consumes — so a model failure plausibly indexes model+serialization limits, not human confusion [2][3][6].

## Falsification attempt
- **Falsifier proposed:** If LLM inference-from-stripped-structure does not correlate with human discoverability — guessing cryptic purposes or failing on easy screens — the probe measures the model, not the interface.
- **Falsifier tested via:** ExaSearch (research_paper) for LLM-vs-human usability correlation and LLM-simulated-user fidelity; ExaContents-level text extraction of the two most on-point papers; repo grep/read of `spaces/page.tsx` + `generate/page.tsx` for a stripping thought-experiment; `curl -I` on the four decisive URLs.
- **Outcome: falsifier largely CONFIRMED for the correlation claim.** The strongest, most direct evidence [2] (first-click prediction = the same "what would a fresh user do here" task) shows GPT diverging from real users in a *majority* of tasks. Heuristic-eval studies [3][5] show low recall + hallucination. The five-second-test backing [1][4] validates the *construct* but only when a *human* views a *rendered* screen — it does not license substituting an LLM reading a stripped tree. The falsifier is **refuted only** for the narrow reachability/punch-list variant, which makes no correlation claim.

## Position-swap reconciliation
- **Supporter reading:** Feasibility is real (structure survives stripping, `spaces/page.tsx:99-188`); the instrument is validated [1]; even imperfect correlation yields a cheap triage signal, and the reachability check is bulletproof.
- **Detractor reading:** The single most relevant study says the model misreads fresh-user intent in 53% of cases [2]; low recall + stochasticity [3][6] mean a "cannot-name-it" verdict is dominated by model/serialization noise; stripping copy discards the very artifact humans judge, so the probe is measuring the wrong thing on the wrong modality.
- **Reconciliation:** Detractor is better supported by fetched evidence. [2] and [3] are empirical and directly on-crux; the supporter's correlation claim rests on an *analogy* to a human-viewing-rendered instrument [1] that the evidence explicitly breaks. The mechanism is feasible and the reachability sub-use is sound, but the headline claim — model-can't-name-it ⇒ human-can't-discover-it — is contradicted, not merely unsupported.

## Verdict
- **Verdict:** REFUTE
- **Confidence:** 0.68
- **Justification:** The riskiest assumption is the whole idea, and the most direct evidence [2] refutes it (LLM fresh-user inference diverges from humans in a majority of cases), reinforced by low-recall/hallucination [3][5] and stochasticity [6]. Confidence is held below 0.8 because (i) the *reachability punch-list* facet genuinely survives and could be salvaged as a separate signal, and (ii) [2] is a recent 0-citation preprint. Not INCONCLUSIVE because the crux was externally grounded and came back negative, not merely untested.

## Constraint for next cycle
- **Constraint text:** Do NOT treat an LLM's inability to name a screen's purpose from a stripped serialization as evidence of human discoverability failure; if the probe is kept, restrict it to the falsifiable, correlation-free reachability check (is every affordance the structure *promises* actually present and reachable) and never claim it predicts human comprehension.
