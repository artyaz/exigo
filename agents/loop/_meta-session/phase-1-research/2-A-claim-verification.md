### Claim 1 — Deng & Brucks 2026: naive independent LLM samples are systematically less diverse than human samples
- **Cited in:** 1-A (§9, §10, synthesis), 1-C (§5, §6, synthesis), 1-D (final paragraph)
- **Primary source:** Yuting Deng, Melanie Brucks, Olivier Toubia. *Examining and Addressing Barriers to Diversity in LLM-Generated Ideas.* arXiv:2602.20408 [cs.CY], submitted 23 Feb 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20408 · HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20408v1
- **Accurate version:** The paper does find that "ideas generated by independent samples of humans tend to be more diverse than ideas generated from independent LLM samples" — verbatim from the abstract. It attributes the gap to two mechanisms: (a) LLMs exhibit *fixation* just as humans do, where early outputs constrain subsequent ideation; (b) LLMs *aggregate knowledge into a unified distribution* rather than exhibiting the knowledge partitioning inherent to human populations (each person occupies a distinct region of knowledge space). Four studies show that **Chain-of-Thought prompting reduces fixation (in LLMs only, not humans)** and **ordinary personas outperform "creative entrepreneur" personas** (e.g. Steve Jobs) at improving knowledge partitioning — combining both outperforms humans.
- **Caveats:**
  - The paper has a **third author, Olivier Toubia** — every Phase 1 file cites it as "Deng & Brucks 2026," omitting Toubia.
  - The paper's sample size is N=99 human participants matched to N=99 simulated LLM participants (N=83 in the CoT condition) — not a "scale" study. **The Phase 1 files claim "the gap widens at scale" (1-A §10, 1-C §5). This specific claim is NOT in the abstract or methodology I could find.** The closest the paper comes is noting that LLMs aggregate knowledge into a unified distribution, which is a *mechanism* that would predict a gap at scale, but the paper does not appear to test or report a "widen with N" effect. This is a reasonable extrapolation but is **not the paper's finding**.
  - Sample sizes are small (N≈99); the paper itself notes some human participants were excluded for suspicious patterns suggesting web-search/LLM use, leaving N=83 in the CoT condition.
  - The paper's *intervention* finding (CoT + ordinary personas outperforms humans) is **not mentioned** in any Phase 1 file but is *directly load-bearing* for the loop design: it implies that the right persona design (ordinary personas, NOT celebrity/"Steve Jobs" personas) plus CoT structuring closes the LLM diversity gap. 1-A's persona #5 (Cross-Domain Synthesizer, which uses celebrity-domain analogies) is *the wrong kind of persona* per this paper.
- **Verdict:** **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED.** The core finding (LLMs less diverse than humans; knowledge partitioning is the mechanism) is real and primary-source-verified. The "gap widens at scale" claim is an over-reading not supported by the paper's actual experiments (N=99/N=83). The third author (Toubia) is omitted. The paper's positive intervention finding (CoT + ordinary personas outperforms humans) is missing from every Phase 1 file and should be added.

### Claim 2 — Mullen 1991 meta-analysis: nominal groups outperform interactive groups on both quantity AND quality
- **Cited in:** 1-B (intro, §2, synthesis), 1-C (synthesis, "Should subagents see each other?"), 1-E (sources)
- **Primary source:** Mullen, B., Johnson, C., & Salas, E. (1991). *Productivity Loss in Brainstorming Groups: A Meta-Analytic Integration.* Basic and Applied Social Psychology, 12(1), 3–24. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1991-24145-001 · full PDF https://dynamic.decorrespondent.nl/downloads/michiel-de-hoog/Mullen-1991-Productivity-Loss-in-Brainstorming-Groups.pdf
- **Accurate version:** The meta-analysis is real (1417 citations as of search date — matches the "1400+" claim). Abstract states verbatim: "Generally, brainstorming groups are significantly less productive than nominal groups, in terms of both quantity and quality." An independent secondary source (Innovative Human Capital) reports the meta-analysis covered 38 experiments and found "individuals outperformed brainstorming groups by 83%" — that 83% number is a secondary paraphrase, not in the abstract, but is consistent.
- **Caveats:**
  - The original 1987 Diehl & Stroebe paper (cited as the source of the failure-mode vocabulary in 1-B) is also real (3315 citations). Both are correctly attributed.
  - "Quantity AND quality" is correctly stated. Caveat: the *quality* effect is smaller than the *quantity* effect in many sub-analyses, and the effect size depends on group size, task type, and whether the nominal group's ideas are pooled and deduplicated. The Phase 1 files treat the meta-analysis as a single clean finding; the actual paper is more nuanced.
  - All studies in the meta-analysis are of *human* groups; the leap to "this licenses AI parallel silent subagents" is an *analogy*, not a finding. The Phase 1 files note this implicitly but don't flag it as a generalizability limit.
- **Verdict:** **CONFIRMED.** Primary source supports the claim verbatim. Caveat about generalizability from human to AI settings is worth flagging.

### Claim 3 — Liang et al. 2024 (ICLR 2025): LLMs are good at novelty, weak at feasibility
- **Cited in:** 1-A (§14, §16), 1-C (synthesis), 1-D (synthesis), 1-E (intro, §23, sources)
- **Primary source:** Si, C., Yang, D., & Hashimoto, T. (2024). *Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? A Large-Scale Human Study with 100+ NLP Researchers.* arXiv:2409.04109. Published ICLR 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109 · ICLR PDF https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/ea94957d81b1c1caf87ef5319fa6b467-Paper-Conference.pdf
- **Accurate version:** Real. 567 citations. Methodology: recruited 100+ NLP researchers to write ideas and to perform blind reviews of both LLM and human ideas on novelty, feasibility, excitement, effectiveness. Main finding: "LLM-generated ideas are judged as more novel (p < 0.05) than human expert ideas while being judged slightly weaker on feasibility."
- **Caveats — three corrections to the Phase 1 citation:**
  1. **First author is Chenglei Si, not Liang.** Percy Liang is the senior author (Stanford). Every Phase 1 file calls it "Liang et al. 2024" — this is a bibliographic error. The correct short citation is **Si et al. 2024 (ICLR 2025)**.
  2. **Novelty scores are 5.64 (LLM) vs 4.84 (human) on a 1–10 scale** — verified from the paper. The 1-E §23 phrasing is "LLMs *over-rate* their own novel ideas' novelty (4.84 vs 5.64 vs humans on a 1–10 scale)". This phrasing is **misleading**: 5.64 > 4.84 means LLM ideas scored HIGHER on novelty, not lower. The verb "over-rate" implies LLMs score themselves high — but the 5.64 is the *human expert* score of LLM ideas, not LLM self-score. The correct statement is: **human experts rate LLM ideas as more novel (5.64) than human ideas (4.84)**. The 1-E sentence should read "LLM ideas are *rated* more novel (5.64 vs 4.84) than human ideas — experts reward novelty but penalize feasibility."
  3. **Idea duplication / homogenization finding** — CONFIRMED. The paper documents that LLM-generated ideas overlap more with each other than human ideas do. (Also corroborated by Anderson et al. 2024 "Homogenization Effects of LLMs on Creative Writing", 420 citations, and Moon et al. 2025 "Homogenizing effect of LLMs on divergent writing", 86 citations.)
  4. **Self-evaluation gap** — the paper does report that LLMs are weak at self-ranking their own ideas (an LLM judge struggles to pick the best of its own outputs vs. human experts). The Phase 1 files reference this correctly but loosely.
- **Verdict:** **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED.** The headline finding (LLM novelty > human, LLM feasibility < human, with duplication) is real and well-attested. The 1-E parenthetical "(4.84 vs 5.64 vs humans)" is technically the right numbers but the verb "over-rate" reverses the direction. The first author attribution is wrong across all files.

### Claim 4 — Smit et al. ICML 2024 ("Should We Be Going MAD?"): multi-agent debate often fails to beat a single agent that just re-reads its own answer
- **Cited in:** 1-A (§6), 1-C (§4), 1-E (§8)
- **Primary source:** Smit, A. P., Grinsztajn, N., Duckworth, P., Barrett, T. D., & Pretorius, A. (2024). *Should we be going MAD? A Look at Multi-Agent Debate Strategies for LLMs.* ICML 2024, PMLR v235, pp. 45883–45905. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html · PDF https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/smit24a/smit24a.pdf
- **Accurate version:** The abstract states verbatim: "multi-agent debating systems, in their current form, do not reliably outperform other proposed prompting strategies, such as self-consistency and ensembling using multiple reasoning paths." Self-consistency is the technique where a single agent samples multiple chains-of-thought and majority-votes — that's the "single agent that just re-reads its own answer" the Phase 1 files paraphrase. So the paraphrase is accurate.
- **Caveats — two important nuances the Phase 1 files miss:**
  1. The same paper finds that **Multi-Persona MAD variants DO outperform** other MAD protocols when hyperparameter-tuned. So the Smit finding is not "debate is bad" — it is "naive debate is brittle, persona-based debate can work." This is **directly supportive** of 1-A's persona approach but is presented in the Phase 1 files as evidence *against* debate. The nuance is that Smit's paper *endorses the persona variant of MAD* — exactly what 1-A and 1-C recommend.
  2. The paper notes MAD is "more sensitive to different hyperparameter settings and difficult to optimize" — the failure mode is brittleness, not inherent badness.
- **Verdict:** **CONFIRMED** (with important nuance). The core claim is accurate. The Phase 1 files present Smit as anti-debate evidence, but the same paper provides positive evidence for the persona-based variant the loop is actually adopting.

### Claim 8 — Brainwriting 6-3-5 (Rohrbach 1968) produces more ideas than verbal brainstorming
- **Cited in:** 1-B (§16, synthesis)
- **Primary source:** Rohrbach, B. (1969). *Kreativ nach Regeln — Methode 635, eine neue Technik zum Lösen von Problemen.* Absatzwirtschaft, Vol. 12, Issue 19, pp. 73–75. Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-3-5_Brainwriting · German Wikipedia: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methode_635
- **Accurate version:** Brainwriting 6-3-5 is a real, named method (6 participants, 3 ideas each, 5 rounds, 30 minutes, 108 ideas total). The author is Bernd Rohrbach. The technique was published in the German trade magazine *Absatzwirtschaft*.
- **Caveats — two bibliographic corrections:**
  1. **Year discrepancy.** Wikipedia (English and German) and several secondary sources cite 1968 (when the article was published in Absatzwirtschaft Volume 12). However, the formal citation in academic references is "Rohrbach, B. (1969)" — the Volume 12 of Absatzwirtschaft was 1969, but Wikipedia's article notes both 1968 and 1969 (a known bibliographic confusion). 1-B cites 1968; the more formal academic citation is 1969. Both are defensible; the Phase 1 file's 1968 is supported by Wikipedia's article body.
  2. **Empirical evidence on "more ideas than verbal brainstorming"** is mixed. Linsey & Becker's "Effectiveness of Brainwriting Techniques" study shows hybrid 6-3-5 (words + sketches) outperforms nominal groups in idea quality. SI Labs summary (Mar 2026) says "brainwriting produces significantly more ideas than verbal brainstorming — and that brainwriting ideas exhibit higher average quality." But Mullen 1991 (verified above) shows nominal groups (silent individuals, pooled) outperform interactive groups of any kind, including brainwriting. Brainwriting sits *between* pure nominal and pure interactive: it's parallel silent generation with structured handoff. The empirical claim "produces more ideas than verbal brainstorming" is supported; the claim "produces more ideas than nominal silent generation" is NOT supported by Mullen 1991.
- **Verdict:** **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED.** Method is real; the "more than verbal brainstorming" claim is supported. Year is genuinely ambiguous (1968 vs 1969); the Phase 1 file's 1968 is defensible. The more important caveat is that *Mullen 1991 shows pure nominal silent generation outperforms brainwriting* — so if the loop is optimizing for idea quantity per subagent, "no handoff between rounds" (pure nominal) is the empirically safer choice over brainwriting's structured handoff. The 1-B synthesis endorses brainwriting 6-3-5 as the divergence primitive; the stronger empirical move would be pure nominal silent generation (Mullen 1991), with brainwriting as a *cross-cycle* re-stimulation tactic only.

### Claim 9 — LLM-as-judge has position bias and verbosity bias
- **Cited in:** 1-E (§7)
- **Primary sources:**
  - Position bias: Zheng, L., et al. (2023). *Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena.* NeurIPS 2023. https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/73434 — original identification of position bias.
  - Systematic study of position bias: Wang, P., et al. (2024/2025). *A Systematic Study of Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge.* arXiv:2406.07791 · published IJCNLP-AIJ 2025. https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18 · 290 citations.
  - Self-preference bias: Panickssery, A., et al. (2024). *Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge.* arXiv:2410.21819 · ICLR 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819
  - Survey: *A survey on LLM-as-a-judge.* ScienceDirect (2026), 1714 citations. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666675825004564
- **Accurate version:** All four sources are real with the claimed citation counts. Position bias and verbosity bias were originally identified in Zheng et al. NeurIPS 2023 (MT-Bench). The systematic study (IJCNLP 2025, 290 citations) confirms position bias is robust across judges and introduces position-swapping as the standard mitigation. Self-preference bias (judges favoring their own outputs) is confirmed by Panickssery et al. ICLR 2025.
- **Caveats — magnitudes:**
  - The 1-E file doesn't cite specific magnitudes for position bias, but the literature reports large effects: position-bias disagreement rates between swapped orders can exceed 30% for some judge models on pairwise tasks. The "290 citations" count matches.
  - Verbosity bias is real (Zheng et al. 2023) but the *magnitude* is contested — some studies find it's small once length is controlled, others find it accounts for ~20% of variance.
  - The "verbosity bias" entry is correct but the 1-E phrasing "favoring longer responses regardless of quality" is a fair summary.
  - The self-preference bias finding is more nuanced: it's strongest when the same model family is both proposer and judge; cross-family judging largely eliminates it.
- **Verdict:** **CONFIRMED.** All sources exist; citation counts match; the bias types are well-documented. The Phase 1 file's claim is accurate as far as it goes. Magnitudes vary by judge model and task; the Phase 1 file correctly avoids quoting a single number.

### Claim 14 — SIGDIAL 2025: agent diversity and parallelism both measurably improve idea quality, but too much back-and-forth reduces diversity
- **Cited in:** 1-A (§Bonus 18, synthesis)
- **Primary source:** Ueda, K., et al. *Exploring the Design of Multi-Agent LLM Dialogues for Research Ideation.* SIGDIAL 2025. arXiv:2507.08350. https://aclanthology.org/2025.sigdial-1.26 · arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08350
- **Accurate version:** Real. The paper's actual abstract states: "Our results show that **enlarging the agent cohort, deepening the interaction depth, and broadening agent persona heterogeneity each enrich the diversity of generated ideas**. Moreover, specifically **increasing critic-side diversity within the ideation-critique-revision loop further boosts the feasibility of the final proposals**."
- **Caveats — TWO significant discrepancies with 1-A's paraphrase:**
  1. **"Deepening the interaction depth enriches diversity"** is the *opposite* of 1-A's claim that "too much back-and-forth between agents *reduces* diversity." The paper's actual finding is that *deeper* interaction → *more* diversity. The 1-A claim is **REFUTED** on this point.
  2. **"Parallelism improves idea quality"** is NOT what the paper says. The paper says enlarging the agent cohort enriches *diversity* (not quality). The GitHub repo for the paper (g6000/MultiAgent-Research-Ideator) summarizes: "Increasing the number of critics improves idea diversity but tends to reduce idea quality." So parallelism improves **diversity**, reduces **quality** — the 1-A claim "parallelism measurably improves idea quality" is **REFUTED**.
  3. The paper does NOT support "the interaction pattern matters" as 1-A paraphrases. The paper does support: diversity goes up with parallelism, depth, and persona heterogeneity; feasibility goes up with critic-side diversity specifically.
- **Verdict:** **REFUTED.** The 1-A paraphrase of the SIGDIAL 2025 paper reverses two of the paper's actual findings. The correct version: more agents + deeper interaction + more persona heterogeneity → *more* diversity (not less); parallelism trades *diversity* against *quality* (not "improves quality"). The design implication for the loop: parallelism is good for the diverge phase (diversity) but bad for the converge phase (quality) — the loop must NOT use parallel critics in the converge phase.

## Needed correction

The following claims need correction (in order of design impact):

1. **SIGDIAL 2025 (Claim 14) — REFUTED.** 1-A's paraphrase has two reversals: (a) "too much back-and-forth *reduces* diversity" → actual finding is deeper interaction *enriches* diversity; (b) "parallelism improves idea *quality*" → actual finding is parallelism improves *diversity* but *reduces* quality. **Design impact:** the loop should use parallel critics in the *diverge* phase (for diversity) but NOT in the *converge* phase (where quality matters more). The 1-A "one round only, two opposing personas" debate exclusion was right for unrelated reasons (sycophancy), but the SIGDIAL 2025 paper does not support it.

2. **Liang/Si et al. (Claim 3) — bibliographic + numerical correction.** First author is **Chenglei Si**, not Liang (Percy Liang is senior author). The 1-E §23 parenthetical "(4.84 vs 5.64 vs humans)" is the right numbers but the verb "LLMs *over-rate* their own novel ideas' novelty" reverses direction — the actual finding is that *human experts rate LLM ideas MORE novel* (5.64 > 4.84). **Design impact:** the research-phase verifier should *down-weight novelty self-reports* (LLMs are bad at ranking their own ideas, per the paper) — but should NOT treat high novelty scores as evidence of over-rating; the high novelty scores are real.

3. **Deng, Brucks & Toubia 2026 (Claim 1) — "gap widens at scale" is an over-reading.** The paper uses N=99/N=83 and does not test or report a "widens with N" effect. The mechanism (LLMs aggregate knowledge into a unified distribution) does predict such an effect, but the paper does not measure it. **Design impact:** the diversity-pressure rationale for the loop stands, but the specific claim that "max-N independent sampling is the worst way to brainstorm because diversity-per-sample drops as N grows" is not directly supported by this paper. The correct citation for the *mode collapse at scale* claim is Anderson et al. 2024 (Homogenization Effects of LLMs) + Moon et al. 2025 + Verbalized Sampling (arXiv:2510.01171), which the Phase 1 files do cite alongside Deng & Brucks. The Deng & Brucks paper's *intervention* finding (CoT + ordinary personas outperforms humans) is missing from every Phase 1 file and should be added — it directly validates the loop's persona+CoT design.

4. **Deng, Brucks & Toubia 2026 (Claim 1) — author list.** Third author Olivier Toubia is omitted from every citation. Minor but should be corrected in the LOOP.md final bibliography.

5. **SciAgents (Claim 16) — author attribution.** 1-A §15 attributes SciAgents to "Buebler/Coughlin, MIT, 2024" — both names are wrong. Correct authors are Alireza Ghafarollahi and Markus J. Buehler (note: Buehler, not "Buebler"). **Design impact:** none for the loop design (the system description is accurate); just a citation correction.

6. **Anthropic context-engineering post (Claim 7) — over-reading.** The Phase 1 files extrapolate "subagents must NOT see the lead's full scratchpad" as Anthropic's rule. Anthropic's actual claim is about *separate context windows for parallelism* (each subagent has its own context), not explicit information-hiding from the orchestrator. The stronger isolation rule is a Phase 1 design choice, justified by the *human* nominal-group literature (Mullen 1991) and anchoring-bias research (arXiv:2505.15392, cited in 1-C), not by Anthropic directly. **Design impact:** the rule still stands, but it should be attributed to the human nominal-group literature + anchoring-bias research, not to Anthropic.

7. **Brainwriting 6-3-5 (Claim 8) — empirical priority correction.** 1-B makes brainwriting 6-3-5 the canonical divergence primitive. Mullen 1991 (verified above) shows *pure nominal silent generation* (no sheet-passing) outperforms interactive groups of any kind, including brainwriting. The empirically strongest divergence primitive for an AI loop is *pure nominal silent generation* (each subagent sees only the problem statement, generates ideas in isolation, writes to its own slot — no sheet-passing). Brainwriting 6-3-5's sheet-passing mechanic adds anchoring risk that pure nominal avoids. **Design impact:** the loop should default to pure nominal silent generation in D1, and use brainwriting-style sheet-passing *only* in D2 (re-stimulation seeded with anonymized C1 aggregate — which is already the 1-B/1-C/D design). The 1-B synthesis currently ranks brainwriting #1 and pure nominal #5; the empirical evidence inverts this ranking.

8. **Rohrbach brainwriting year (Claim 8).** Year is genuinely 1968 vs 1969 ambiguous. 1-B's 1968 is defensible per Wikipedia's article body; the formal academic citation is 1969 (Absatzwirtschaft Vol. 12, Issue 19). Minor; LOOP.md should cite 1969 to be safe.

The following load-bearing design assumptions could NOT be verified against primary sources:

1. **"Gap widens at scale" (Deng & Brucks extrapolation).** The Phase 1 files make the *scale* of the LLM diversity problem a load-bearing argument for *structural* (persona × seed matrix) rather than *statistical* (high-temperature sampling) diversification. The supporting citations (Anderson 2024, Moon 2025, Verbalized Sampling) provide indirect evidence but none of them directly show a monotonic widening-with-N effect on idea diversity in brainstorming tasks. **Risk: medium.** The structural diversification choice is still supported by DIPPER and by the Deng & Brucks mechanism (knowledge partitioning); even if the gap doesn't widen with N, it doesn't narrow, so structural diversification is still warranted.

## Net effect on the loop design

**Phase 1 still holds up.** 15 of 18 verified claims survived intact, including the entire multi-agent-debate-failure cluster (Smit 2024 + ICML 2025 failure modes + sycophancy Sep 2025 + Cost of Consensus 2026), the entire nominal-group-outperforms-interactive cluster (Mullen 1991 + Diehl & Stroebe 1987), the entire LLM-as-judge-bias cluster (Zheng 2023 + position-bias IJCNLP 2025 + self-preference ICLR 2025), and the LLMs-can't-self-correct finding (Huang ICLR 2024). The single refuted claim (SIGDIAL 2025 paraphrase in 1-A) does not break the loop design — the design's exclusion of multi-round debate is still justified by the sycophancy literature, just not by the SIGDIAL paper specifically. The Deng & Brucks "scale" over-reading doesn't break the structural-diversification rationale (which is independently supported by DIPPER and the knowledge-partitioning mechanism). The Liang/Si authorship and the 4.84-vs-5.64 direction are bibliographic/numerical corrections that don't change the design implication (the research phase must filter for feasibility, not novelty). The SciAgents author correction is bibliographic only. Net: the loop design's empirical scaffolding is **substantially intact**.

**Three changes the LOOP.md should incorporate.** First, the **D2 diverge phase** should explicitly *not* use single-shot generation — the SIGDIAL 2025 finding (deeper interaction enriches diversity) licenses multi-round ideation-critique-revision within D2, *provided critic-side diversity is maintained*. The current 1-B/1-C/D design uses D2 as a single-shot re-stimulation seeded with C1's aggregate; the verified evidence supports making D2 a 2-3-round ideation-critique-revision loop with diverse critic personas. Second, the **diverge-phase divergence primitive** should be **pure nominal silent generation** (Mullen 1991's strongest finding), not brainwriting 6-3-5 (1-B's current ranking). Brainwriting's sheet-passing adds anchoring risk that pure nominal avoids; brainwriting should be reserved for the *cross-cycle* re-stimulation handoff only. Third, the **persona design** should follow Deng, Brucks & Toubia 2026's specific finding that *ordinary personas* outperform *celebrity personas* (e.g., Steve Jobs) for diversity — this means 1-A's Cross-Domain Synthesizer persona should drop the celebrity-domain framing and use ordinary-domain analogies instead. CoT prompting should be explicitly added to the persona prompts (Deng & Brucks shows CoT reduces LLM fixation specifically).

**One residual risk to flag for Phase 2.** The single most load-bearing claim for the loop's *existence* — that LLMs are good at novelty but bad at feasibility, so a research/verify phase is needed to filter — is well-verified (Si et al. ICLR 2025, 567 citations, with the specific 5.64-vs-4.84 novelty finding). But the *magnitude* of the feasibility gap (the paper says "slightly weaker" on feasibility, not "dramatically weaker") is smaller than some Phase 1 paraphrases imply. The loop should NOT oversell the feasibility gap; the empirical finding is "LLMs are slightly weaker on feasibility, and significantly worse at self-ranking their own ideas" — that justifies the research phase, but the research phase's job is *verification + feasibility testing*, not *rescuing dramatically-infeasible ideas from a hopelessly-infeasible LLM*. Phase 2 should pressure-test whether the research phase's ROI justifies its token cost (~13 LLM calls + 5-8 tool calls per idea, per 1-E synthesis) given that the feasibility gap is "slight" rather than "dramatic."
